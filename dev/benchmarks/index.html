Benchmarks

Times of the complete computation of the elementary divisors of the matrices below, on four machines. For the package, the time is that of the whole call to sparse_elementary_divisors on the matrix already read from its file: reduction, extraction of the core, and Hecke on the core. For Magma, it is the time that Magma measures for ElementaryDivisors alone, without the transfer of the matrix. For Hecke alone, it is the time of Hecke.elementary_divisors on the matrix already built as a SMat. Each time is the median of three runs, unless stated otherwise. The measurements were made in November 2026 with version 0.1.1 of the package, Julia 1.13.3, Hecke 0.41.1 and Magma V2.29-4, by the script benchmark/benchmarks.jl of the repository, where the raw results are kept (benchmark/results).

Matrices

The matrices G₃₇ d₃ to d₆ are the differentials of the reduced lcm resolution of the dual braid monoid of the complex reflection group G₃₇ (a variant of Dehornoy–Lafont's resolution with fewer cells, see the Bibliography), produced by the preliminary implementation of GarsideHomology.jl. The matrices ZT2w4m5 d₂ to d₄ were provided by Najib Idrissi (see the acknowledgements). They are not distributed with the package for now. The core is the matrix left after the elimination of the unit pivots, which Hecke then reduces.

The package runs wherever Julia runs: the other tables give its times on server 1, with Hecke alone for comparison, and on two workstations.

Comparison with Magma

On server 2: Magma, which uses one thread by default, and the package with one thread and with all 16 threads. Both computations have two phases. For a sparse matrix, Magma's ElementaryDivisors first performs a sparse elimination with Markowitz pivoting, then passes the remaining matrix to its algorithm for dense matrices, usually a modular one (see the chapter Sparse Matrices and the section Canonical Forms of the Magma handbook). The package eliminates the unit pivots, then passes the remaining matrix to Hecke. In both cases, the matrix passed to the second phase is called the core below.

Magma's ElementaryDivisors draws random numbers (the step of the random number generator, given by GetSeed, advances by millions during the call), from a seed that Magma chooses at random when it starts. Its core, and with it its time and its memory, change from one run to the next, as its verbose output shows (SetVerbose("SparseMatrix", 1)): on ZT2w4m5 d₄, the time of Magma ranges from 21 min 51 s to 56 min 17 s, and its peak memory, in the runs where it was measured, from 40 GiB to more than 59 GiB; two runs out of eight were stopped by the system for lack of memory. With a fixed seed (magma -S 1, see Random Object Generation in the Magma handbook), the computation is reproducible: on ZT2w4m5 d₄, 24 to 25 min and 26 GiB with this seed. The package draws no random numbers: its core is the same on every run, on every machine and for any number of threads. Its peak memory on ZT2w4m5 d₄ is 30 to 32 GiB on server 2.

The core of Magma depends on the seed: the table gives it for magma -S 1, from the verbose output, in the orientation of the original matrix. The two eliminations choose different pivots and stop at different points. On ZT2w4m5 d₂, Magma stops when the density of the remaining matrix exceeds 50%, while the package continues. On ZT2w4m5 d₄, after almost the same number of pivots (431161 for Magma, 431162 for the package), the core of Magma has 2.8 million entries and that of the package 130 million.
